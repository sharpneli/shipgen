# Handoff notes for the next session

Read README.md first: it documents the inputs, outputs and conventions. This file covers what the README
doesn't: the decisions behind the current design, how to work safely here, and what's still open.

## Decisions the user made (don't undo them without asking)
- **Style vs type:** `style` is the design family (warship, carrier, merchant, planing). `type` (BB, BC, DD, ...) is only a gameplay label. Battlecruisers and destroyers are warships.
- **Arcs are fixed by mount kind** (`hitbox.ARC_*`), never deduced from obstructions. The user wanted no fiddling.
  - End turrets: ±135°. Side mounts, including wing turrets: ±90°.
  - Side-firing centreline turrets (flush turrets behind a superfiring one, amidships turrets): ±65° about each beam.
  - Fixed MTB tubes: ±1°.
- **Every mount exports `traverse_deg` (user, 2026-10-05: "I want the turret movement logic in the actual game have an easy time"):** the one interval it turns within, never wrapping, so the game can't train barrels into the bridge or a partner turret. `hitbox.mount_traverse` is the one rule, and `Layout.reserve_sweep` reserves exactly it. That replaced "arcs plus the turn from rest to the starboard arc": side-firing centreline turrets now reserve both turns to the beams; only all_forward_flush moved (+0.4 t).
- **Side-firing turrets stow fore-and-aft** like real ships, so their rest bearing is outside their arcs. The game trains them out before firing.
  - Flush end-group turrets keep stowing inboard, toward the ship (user, 2026-10-05). Stowing them outboard like the rest of their group, as Dreadnought's X did, was tried and dropped. Their muzzles then had to clear the turret ahead, which cost Dreadnought 184 → 190 m and +712 t standard. The user accepts the visual difference to keep that weight down.
- **Secondaries stow toward the nearer end (user, 2026-10-05, from my research note):** at rest a side gun trains toward the nearer end of the ship (amidships, x >= 0, counts as forward) and stops at the edge of its arc (`armament.stow_bearing`). Deck secondaries (±90° about their beam) therefore lie fore-and-aft, like Iowa's 5 in or Bismarck's 15 cm in harbour photos, and casemates (±60°) lie along the hull side, 30° off the centreline. Elevation is level (we don't model it). Arcs are unchanged: side mounts (`side_mount`) and casemates now centre their arc on their own beam instead of on the rest bearing. Side torpedo mounts still stow on the beam, and the hand-authored fleet.py ships are untouched.
  - Stowed barrels take deck room. `add_mount` books a barrel footprint (`barrel_footprint`, in a height band about the axis) for `side_mount` guns. Both the warship deck battery and `side_pairs` (carriers, merchants, planing) keep a mount its barrels' length from the next one in the direction they point (`pitch_stowed`). They also need its barrels clear of what stands at their height and of main-turret sweeps; if the even spread breaks that, they fall back to the free-spot search. Result: no design changed size. all_forward is 0.2 m wider, and Bismarck 79 t and the heavy cruiser 6 t lighter, because AA and boats picked other spots. `verify.py` now checks that each secondary's stowed barrels (past the root) hit nothing at their axis height.
- **Wing turrets fire across the deck only when asked** (`main.cross_deck`, echelon pairs only; user, 2026-10-05: "Invincible and Seydlitz are calling"). Without it, that's their deliberate limitation. With it, each gets a second arc of ±30° about the far beam (`hitbox.ARC_CROSS`; my pick between Invincible's blast-limited few tens of degrees and the German long-stagger ships' 100+), and trains across through the end it stows toward (`hitbox.cross_turn`); the partner usually blocks the other way (Invincible; Seydlitz's stands just out of reach), and only one way is reserved. The layout reserves the whole swing (`reserve_sweep`), widens the stagger so the partner's body stays out of the cross arc, and keeps plan items on the turn side beyond the muzzles (`wing_side`); the bridge and aft control past the plan's ends get that as deck-only room (`cross_ends`), so machinery running on under them can fill it. Invincible uses it (194 → 206 m). New example `seydlitz.json` (kiel `great_war`; A, an echelon cross-deck pair, two superfiring aft, 150 mm casemates and 88 mm on deck): 223.5 × 27.5 m, 29.4k t std, against the real 200.6 × 28.5 m and 25k t, so it runs long and heavy like the other early-turbine ships (Kongo, Invincible). By default each fires only on its own side, from dead ahead to dead astern (`main.wing` pairs, `main.echelon`), and stows fore-and-aft at the edge of that arc. Wing turrets forward of amidships can therefore join the forward group's fire: A plus a wing pair gives 3 guns ahead, and A, B plus a pair gives 4. The layout keeps their muzzles short of the end groups' inner turrets.
- **Spare length goes to the ends (user, 2026-10-05):** the citadel runs from end group to end group, and the belt should be as short as possible, so it must never grow for crew space (crew may use spare room inside it, but quarters belong in the unprotected ends). When the hull is longer than the middle needs (crew, fuel, the speed rule), `shipdesign.spread_ends` gives the end clearances as much of the spare as they take (`build_layout(spread=)`, split in proportion to the preferred bow and stern clearances), bisected for the largest spread that leaves the layout no worse: nothing short, no new errors or layout warnings. Sizing still runs at spread 0, so lengths and beams don't change; positions, citadel length and weights do. The check is needed because the middle's length need (`M_req`) doesn't count everything standing there (torpedo mounts beside a single funnel, deck secondaries beside wing turrets, wing-pair room, deckhouse crew volume); an uncapped rule without it grew the destroyer 130 → 149.5 m. Seen against the old layout: destroyer A 0.14 → 0.23 L from the bow, heavy cruiser 14.7k → 13.2k t std, battleship 45.6k → 41.9k t. Open: the beam is still fitted at spread 0, so lightened ships come out stiffer (battleship GM 2.01 → 2.79).
- **Crew weight is lumped (user, 2026-10-05):** the game is about combat, and at battle stations the quarters are empty, so the hull-quartered crew stays one weight amidships rather than following its quarters. Outside a fight we handwave that the ship is fine.
- **Warships are built around their guns.** Main turrets are placed first and reserve their barrel sweep (`Layout.reserve_sweep`). Superstructure placed later must keep out of it.
- **Carriers and merchants have no main battery.** All their guns are secondaries fitted where they suit, and overlap is acceptable. Planing craft still have a main battery; the user hasn't decided whether MTB guns should become secondaries.
- **The generator is permissive.** Gameplay limits will live in the game's designer UI. `--no-limits` skips the input ranges entirely. Silly designs may look stupid or fail the physics, but they must run.
- **Looks are visual only** (`"look"` in the design, `looks.py`). The user wants ships of different powers to look different with no gameplay effect: a look may change only the sprite images, never the layout, physics, report results, hitboxes or `sprite.json`. Turret drawings and silhouettes (bow, stern, funnels, superstructure corners, masts) differ by look, but hitboxes always use the layout's shapes. A look's hull may only be fuller than the layout's, never finer, so deck-edge fittings stay on deck. **Looks are indexed by navy and era** (`"look": {"navy", "era"}`, `looks.NAVIES[navy]["eras"][era]`; user, 2026-10-03), because a navy's ships should change over time. A navy may change anything between eras, turret drawings and hull shapes included, and navies differ freely. A missing pair is drawn as `generic` in that era. Navies are named after dockyards: `generic`, `brooklyn` (US), `kure` (Japan), `portsmouth` (UK), `kiel` (Germany), `la_spezia` (Italy), `toulon` (France). Eras (user approved the list, 2026-10-03): `victorian` (1885–1903), `great_war` (1904–1920), `treaty` (1920–1936), `wwii` (1937–1946) and `cold_war` (1950–1970). Every navy has all five. The game picks the era (its calendar, a refit), not the design's physics: `render_ship(look={"era": ...})` repaints.
- **Design and drawing are separate, with a plain-data contract** (`shipdesign.build(design) -> ship` dict; `render.render_ship(ship, ...)`). The user's game calls the design side on every knob change in its designer UI and needs it fast; the preview may lag. Keep the rules: the design side (shipdesign, navarch, layout, armament, hitbox, styles) uses the standard library only and never imports the renderer; the renderer (render, shipgen, shadow, looks) imports no design-side module and reads only the `ship` dict; `geometry.py` is the shared layer. Colours live only in `looks.py`. A physics refactor should change only the design side, and a drawing change only the render side.
- **Barrels show only partly (user, 2026-10-03):** sprites and hitboxes draw a fixed fraction of `barrel_len` from the barrel root (`geometry.BARREL_SHOWN`): 0.8 for turrets (`bb`, `dp`) and 0.5 for casemates. The rest is taken as inside the mount and isn't modelled at all. Open mounts, torpedo tubes and fixed tubes show their full length. `barrel_len` in the turret type stays the real length; ballistics use the real length in calibres. The sweep reservation uses the shown length, so this shortened gun-heavy ships (Nassau 160 → 153.5 m, Dreadnought 187.5 → 184 m) and changed the hand-authored fleet's turret sprites.
- **Superfire step after real ships (user, 2026-10-04):** each superfiring tier stands `geometry.superfire_step(th)` = `2.0 + 0.2 × turret height` above the one it fires over (was `th + 1.0`, which made big-gun stacks, especially three-tier all-forward, far too tall). It's about one deck for every calibre, a little more for big turrets: Iowa's 16in turret II stands 2.6 m over turret I, and Atlanta's 5in twins step up about a deck (2.4 m) each. Small mounts must keep a full deck's step; a formula scaled purely on turret height made Atlanta-style stacks look squat. Layout, armament and the barbette weight in `navarch.mount_weights` all use it.
- **Designer inputs are tickboxes and multiple choice (user, 2026-10-05):** no tuned lengths. Each choice is independent, so any combination gives a valid ship: a missing anchor falls back with a warning, never an error, and the layout moves things instead of failing. Players tune only toward a size target. Limits stay, as validation ranges. Historical behaviour that followed geometry becomes a rule; a knob only where practice varied widely.
- **Raised decks (user, 2026-10-05; reverses the 2026-10-04 "no raised forecastle"):** `hull.raised` lists stretches `{from: bow|stern, to: fore_group|bridge|funnels|aft_control|aft_group, decks}`, combined by taking the highest deck at each point. `hull.freeboard` stays the main deck's. Deck numbers stay internal (−1, −2 … above the main deck); period names are the UI's job. Whether a raised stretch carries the girder is a rule by geometry (its mean height over the middle 0.4 L), not a knob, since historically it followed from length and position.
- Shadows come from the height map, not baked in. Mips are packed per layer (`<layer>_mips.png`, rects in `sprite.json`).
- **One static hull image (user, 2026-10-05):** `hull.png` replaced `hull_base.png` + `hull_upper.png`. The split dates from the first commit, when shadows were baked in, and only sandwiched the turrets between low and tall structure. With the height map it bought nothing: the height map holds one height per pixel, as one image holds one colour, so no occlusion the game can show is lost. Turrets now draw over the whole hull. A turret's cast shadow falls only where `H < top_m` (`shadow.below_mask`; it used the upper layer's alpha before). The `layer` key on spec items survives as the draw pass inside `hull.png` (a roof must not draw over what stands on it). The merged image matches the old composite within 4/255 on antialiased edges; report and hitboxes are unchanged. Visible change: turrets over mast legs (Dreadnought, Nassau), and the seaplane carrier's aft 76 mm, which stands on the hangar roof, was drawn under that roof before.
- **Turret draw order from height (user, 2026-10-05):** a mount's `z` in `sprite.json` is its rank by base height, main over secondary over the rest at equal height (`layout.draw_order`, run in `finish_layout`). Before, each call site passed a constant (main `1 + level`, wing/midships main 1, deck secondaries 3, casemates 0), so secondaries drew over flush, first-tier superfiring and all wing/midships main turrets, barrels included, while the hitboxes had them underneath (seen on Invincible's deckhouse wing turrets). `add_mount` and `side_pairs` no longer take `z`. Only `sprite.json` z values changed; reports and hitboxes are identical.
- **No period-based physics (user, 2026-10-03).** Nothing is weighed or limited by era: 10 t of armour weighs 10 t whenever it was designed. Differences between periods come from material science, given in the design as numbers like `machinery.tech`. For armour, the material (`armour.materials`, a plain string passed through to the hitboxes) sets its protective value in the game's ballistics; mass stays thickness × area × density. There are no artificial period limits. Armour schemes such as all-or-nothing or incremental aren't rules here either: the inputs only describe where the armour is, and a separate game system will handle design styles.
- **Mip atlases are an interchange format only.** The game uploads each level to the GPU separately (Vulkan mip layout is hardware dependent) and never samples the packed image, so the missing gutters don't matter. Large sprites are fine too: the game may drop the biggest mip levels.

- **Powerplant (done; `powerplant.py`, `research/powerplant-model.md`, README "Design input").** All four steps of the session plan are in:
  1. plant size and weight from the tech
  2. the machinery block with rooms and bunkers
  3. funnels from gas flow and uptake reach, with midships turrets only in gaps next to the boilers
  4. smoke on control positions, and the published plant numbers (report `plant`)
  - The user's decisions:
    - There is no year input: designs carry the tech as numbers (`machinery.tech`), because players may research things early or late.
    - `plant-templates.md`, written by `plant_templates.py`, has example blocks by year for authoring designs.
    - The spec is "not taken 1:1". Ships getting longer and looking different as requirements are added is fine.
  - Departures from the spec:
    - Boiler rooms use the whole height to the bounding deck. Only engine rooms keep the spec's "unit height + 2.5 m" cap, so low turbines no longer make boiler rooms long. With that, the spec's own densities (ST7 0.34, ST8 0.36) give sensible WWII lengths, so they're unchanged.
    - The forward boiler group may run on under the bridge (`layout.BRIDGE_OVER_BOILERS`, 0.85 of its length). Its funnels stay on open deck.
    - Funnel casings are drawn at 3× the gas area (`powerplant.CASING`).
    - Warship funnels are trunked aft (user, 2026-10-03): each boiler group's funnels slide toward the boiler group's aft boundary (usually the engine rooms), centring on it if they can. The slide stops at the uptake reach: each end of the group must stay within `reach_m` + half a funnel of a funnel, or no farther than planned. Turret sweeps, the aft control and other funnels stop it too. Grouped plants (boilers forward, engines about twice as long aft) otherwise crowded the funnels into the forward third of the machinery, and players read the ships as nose-heavy, though trim was already balanced. WWII plants (reach about 29 m) move most. Short-reach coal plants (Mikasa, Dreadnought) stay over their boilers, and Nassau and Connecticut move part way, which puts more smoke on their aft control. Kongo, Invincible and the battlecruiser can't move, because their Q turret stands on the boundary. Centring short-reach groups on the boundary is impossible with any funnel count: the forward funnel would be at least half the group's length from the front boilers. The longer uptakes are weighed. Carriers (island) and merchants (house) are unchanged.
    - Coal wing bunkers run up to the main deck.
    - Oil also fills the torpedo protection's liquid layers.
    - Funnels never limit arcs. They compete through sweep reservation instead, since arcs stay fixed.
    - Natural and boost draught plants get funnel tops 25 m above the grates (`layout.STACK_NATURAL`).
  - **Calibration (as of 2026-10-02):**
    - Most designs now land within about 10% of the real ships' lengths: Nassau 159.5 m, Mikasa 122.5 m, Connecticut 128 m, the battleship 257 m, the heavy cruiser 200 m, the fleet carrier 245.5 m.
    - Early turbine ships run 15–20% long: Dreadnought 185 m against 160 m, Invincible 192 m against 172 m, Kongo 257 m against 214 m.
    - Most of the rest is cruise fuel: early direct turbines are very inefficient at cruise, so Kongo carries 8,800 t, much of it in end bunkers. That's left for the fuel-range tuning the user plans, together with hull width (beam preference).
  - Not done yet:
    - Generator rooms for electric transmission aren't placed separately.
    - The cruise model has no cruising-turbine choice (the template has a "with cruising turbines" variant).
    - Fuel tonnage drives bunker length, but coal's protective value isn't modelled (that's the game's).
- **Crew (done; `crew.py`, `research/crew-space-model.md`, README "Design input").** The user's decisions:
  - Standards are numbers in the design. The six spec standards are only template blocks (`crew-templates.md`). In the game a slider sets them, and the crew's mood is simulated smoothly from what the ship has.
  - `endurance_days` is its own input. A ship may loiter far longer than its fuel range (a tender). Shorter than the range is silly but only warns.
  - Crew lives in any empty volume, hull or superstructure. At this scale, too narrow for real people is fine.
  - Implementation:
    - The complement is departmental: plant, guns, deck and command, air group, then hotel.
    - Volume is checked against `crew.USABLE` × empty volume. Shortfall is a "length" need, so the hull grows.

## How to work here
- **Git:** the repo is on GitHub (`git@github.com:sharpneli/shipgen.git`, branch `main`). Pushing over SSH works with the user's key. Commit or push only when the user asks.
- Dependencies are `pip install cairosvg pillow numpy`. The system Python lacks them; use the venv at `~/.venv` (`~/.venv/bin/python design.py ...`).
- **Checking the boundary:** `python3 -c "import shipdesign, json; shipdesign.build(json.load(open('designs/battleship.json')))"` must work with the system Python (no PIL, cairosvg or numpy), and importing `render` must not load `shipdesign`, `navarch`, `layout`, `styles`, `hitbox` or `armament` (check `sys.modules`).
- **Speed:** measured 2026-10-05, `shipdesign.build` takes about 15 ms (planing craft) to 1.9 s (Dreadnought) per ship; most warships take 0.3–1.7 s, all 29 designs about 16 s in series. The size search runs the layout around 10–25 times, and `spread_ends` adds a bisection and a rebalance (about 15–35%). Dreadnought took 14.6 s until `polygons_intersect` learned to test only edges inside the two shapes' shared bounding box: its 488-point forecastle outline was tested edge by edge against every deckhouse probe. A `hint` (the previous length) halves that. `Layout.free` and `geometry.polygons_intersect` reject by bounding box first, and AA spacing checks only the AA footprints. `render_ship(previews=False)` takes about 0.5 s; the previews (numpy shadow march), the sheet and the debug overlay are most of the ~3 s full render.
- **Checking a look change:** render a design in every navy and era and confirm `hitboxes.json`, `sprite.json` and `report.json` (except its `inputs` echo) are identical across looks. `standard` must stay byte-identical to the old sprites.
- **Regenerate `out_designs/` at milestones** (`~/.venv/bin/python design.py designs/*.json --no-limits`) and commit it. The user reads the outputs there. Not needed after every change (user, 2026-10-04): compare in a scratch folder meanwhile, and refresh everything when a piece of work wraps up. **At the end of any large piece of work, before committing, regenerate all of `out_designs/` with 12 processes at once** (user, 2026-10-05): `ls designs/*.json | xargs -P 12 -I{} ~/.venv/bin/python design.py {} --no-limits`, then `verify.py out_designs/*`, then commit and push.
- **Regression method used throughout:** copy `out_designs/` aside, regenerate, and `diff -r`. Unrelated designs should stay byte-identical; expected changes should be limited to the designs you meant to change. The hand-authored fleet (`python shipgen.py --out <dir>`) has stayed byte-identical through all the changes except the barrel-shown change (2026-10-03) and the deck margin planks (2026-10-05, user: every deck, main and raised, gets a margin plank round its edge, and raised decks are planked as their own decks), so keep it that way.
- Run `python verify.py out_designs/*` after every change. It does pixel checks of sprites against hitboxes.
- **Look at the 3D hitbox views** (`hitbox_*.png`, `hitview.py`) after hitbox changes. They caught compartments sticking out of the hull and barbettes hanging under sponsons.
  - Cells reach the hull's widest point over their section; the views clip them to the hull outline.
  - The 3D views draw every cell. The space-filling rooms (quarters, stores, double bottom, torpedo protection; `hitview.FILLER`) are faint, so the user can see the whole state at a glance: coal wing bunkers outside the machinery, and the citadel's length from the belt and its armoured end bulkheads (dark slabs).
  - `hitbox_cells.png` shows every tier of the subdivision in plan. `verify.py` checks the subdivision: one owning room per cell, points in the hull in exactly one cell, mutual neighbours, and magazine links.
  - Barbettes reach the armour deck only for mounts inside the hull and below any flight deck.
- `verify.py` checks every mount's `traverse_deg` (one interval under a full turn, holding the rest and every arc, the same in `sprite.json`) and swings each main turret's barrels through it against everything taller than their axis (superstructure, funnels, other turrets at rest). Tried by hand, it caught a cross-deck turret turned the blocked way and a superfiring turret turned through the bridge.
- The user edits files in `designs/` themselves. Never overwrite their designs. As of the end of this session:
  - `destroyer.json` has `calibre_mm: 1270`, probably a deliberate silly test. Sized, it comes out at 418 m and 163k t.
  - `murica.json` has block coefficient 0.34 (needs `--no-limits`).
  - The user asked for `length` and `beam` to be removed from every design. That was done on 2026-10-02, and nothing else in the designs was touched.
- **Adding a key to every design:** the designs are hand-formatted JSON. Edit only the substring of the object you change (find `"armour": {`, match the braces, `json.loads` it, check `json.dumps` reproduces it exactly, then splice the new dump back in). `gangut.json` is multi-line, so insert lines there instead. Never re-dump whole files: it would reformat the user's designs.
- Output folders are named after the design's `id`, not its file name. Duplicate ids overwrite each other; this already happened once.

## Known gaps and ideas
- **Deferred by the user (2026-10-05), to pick up later:**
  - **Beam refit after the spread:** `size` fits the beam with the spare length amidships (spread 0); `spread_ends` then lightens armoured ships (shorter citadel) on that beam, so they come out stiffer (battleship GM 2.01 → 2.79, heavy cruiser 1.31 → 1.97). Fit the beam again at the chosen spread.
  - **Pitch and yaw gyradius in the report:** nothing computes the moment of inertia yet. Every weight has an x (and z), so it's cheap; the game could turn and pitch by mass distribution, rewarding a compact heavy middle.
  - **`M_req` (the middle's length need) undercounts:** it leaves out torpedo mounts beside a single funnel, deck secondaries beside wing turrets, wing-pair room and deckhouse crew volume. `spread_ends`' check covers for it now; counting them properly would let the ends take all the spare without the bisection.
  - **Crew fills the citadel first (optional):** quarters are spread over the free cells by volume, citadel or not; free citadel cells could fill before the ends.
  - **Torpedo placement needs a full check and verification:** large batteries in the spirit of the Japanese heavy cruisers (the user's example: 3 × 8 launchers per side; check the real ships' numbers when this is picked up), placement beside and between funnels, deck-edge swing room, and how the layout reserves their room.
- **Physics calibration:** carriers' full loads run light; the PT boat runs heavy; the T2 tanker needs about 30% more power than real.
- **No sweep reservation outside warships.** That's deliberate for carriers and merchants (see above).
- **Height map:** columns only, so mast yards, derricks and barrels are left out. It's 8-bit with a 0.25 m step, so anything above 63.75 m clips.
- **Baked lighting:** the light rim on the upper-left edges of blocks and funnels is still baked in.
- **No aircraft are drawn** on carriers; the game is assumed to spawn them.
- **Wing turrets on the main deck** (`amidships_stands_on: "deck"`) may overlap the bridge's level-1 core where they stand next to the bridge. The size search hasn't produced it on Dreadnought, so it hasn't been handled.
- **Missing turrets:** a mount that doesn't fit (beam too narrow, deck taken) is left off the drawing and listed as an error, even with `--no-limits`.
